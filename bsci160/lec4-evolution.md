# Evolution

## Introduction
- Evolution is the change in allele frequency over time
- Adaptive evolution produces *adaptations*, being traits that confer an advantage in an environment
    - Adaptations increase fitness and the chances of survival to reproduction
    - Surviving to reproduction increases the likelihood of one's genes being passed down to future generations
- Causes of evolution:
    - Non-adaptive:
        - Mutation
        - Gene flow + migration
        - Genetic drift
    - Sometimes adaptive:
        - Non-random mating
    - Adaptive:
        - Natural selection
- Natural selection consists of 4 principles:
    - Heritable variation: Differences in traits that can be inherited by offspring
    - Individuals struggle and compete for resources
    - Differential reproductive success: Individuals with beneficial traits are more likely to reproduce and pass on their genes
    - Over time, evolution occurs: Allele frequencies change over many generations, with such changes being driven by "selection"
- Natural selection can lead to adaptation
    - Over time, the population evolves to be better suited to its environment
    - Ecology (temperature, competition, soil moisture, etc.)
- Variation must be heritable
- Types of selection:
    - Directional: The population average shifts toward a specific spot
    - Stabilizing selection: Selection against both extremes occurrs, meaning that members of the population move towards the average
    - Disrupting selection: The opposite of stabilizing selection in that the extremes are selected for relative to the mean
