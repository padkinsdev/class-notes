# Species and Speciation

## Classification
- Classification of species is done in a hierarchical manner (taxonomic rank), going from broadest to most specific (individual species). The order is domain -> kingdom -> phylum -> class -> family -> genus -> species
- Naming a species takes the form \<capitalized genus name\> \<lowercase species name\>

### Biological Species Concept
- Most widely used species concept
- States that all members have the potential to interbreed under natural conditions and produce viable, fertile offspring
- Limitations:
    - Can be difficult to apply because it's not always clear who has the potential to interbreed
    - Not applicable to asexual organisms
    - Can't be applied to fossils
    - Boundaries are arbitrary!
    - It's not always clear how much hybridization is too much
- A challenge for the BSC: Ring Species
    - Salamanders around the San Joaquin Valley vary in appearance
    - SOme of the forms interbreed and some don't
    - Differences accumulate at the other end of the geographic area

### Morphological/Phenetic Species COncept
- Based on the similarity of physical traits
- Can be used for extinct or asexual species because it's not reliant on reproductive isolation
- Can be misleading in cases of convergent evolution or non-visible differences

### Genetic CLuster Species Concept
- Groups of individuals that form distinct genetic clusters with few or no intermediates between other such clusters (based on absence of gene flow)
- Usually 2.5% difference is the threshold

### Ecological Species Concept
= Species have separate roles/requirements in their environments
- Example: Parasitic roundworms in humans and pigs, which are sometimes considered one species given new information that both types can infect either host but are preferential to one type

### Species Complexes
- Sometimes a clean classification isn't possible. Such cases are called species complexes
- Example: Skipper (Astraptes) butterflies
- Boundaries between species are unclear

## Classification complexities
- There are often novel tiers between the regular tiers of a species' taxonomic rank
