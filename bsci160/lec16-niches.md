# Niches

## Introduction

- A niche is one or more of the following:
  - The range of resources and conditions allowing the species to maintain a viable population
  - The interrelation of a species with all the abiotic and biotic factors affecting it
  - Defining a organism's role within an ecosystem: its food, shelter, and its behavioral role
- One dimension can be measured in a species response curve
  - A species response curve graphs a species' abundance against an environmental gradient
  - The peak of the curve represents the optimal condition (optimum zone). The areas without global maxima that still have abundance above zero are the tolerance zones
  - Obviously multiple species' response curves can be graphed together as long as the species abundance is normalized and the environmental gradient factor is the same
  - Adding dimensions to the graph allows for graphing across more than one environmetal factor (gradient)
    - Alternatively, a two-dimensional graph with two environmental factors (on the x and y axes, respectively) can also display species abundance as a oval/sphere

## Competition

### Paramecium

- Paramecium are a genus of heterotrophic ciliates (unicellular protists)
- When Gauss cultured Paramecium species in test tubes, together or separately, aurelia outcompeted caudatum when the two were cultured together, whereas caudatum and bursaria managed to coexist when cultured together
- Conclusions:
  - Presence of a stronger competitor always reduces population growth rate of the weaker competitor
  - When two species coexist, they will both have lower population densities than they would if alone (even though both species survive)

### Competitive exclusion principle

- Also called the limiting similarity principle
- The principle states that two species with identical niches cannot coexist

### Resource partitioning

- Under this principle, species alter their niche and divide resources as opposed to driving each other to extinction

### Fundamental vs. realized niche

- The fundamental niche for a species consists of the factors necessary for it to survive
- In the presence of another species with an overlapping fundamental niche, the first species may retreat to a subset of its fundamental niche (called the realized niche)
  - Other factors may also impact the disparity between a species' fundamental and realized niche, such as presence/absence of hosts

## Takeaways

- Niches determine where a species can live, and species interactions can further limit species' distributions
- Character displacement: Competition drives populations of two species living in the same area to evolve different traits
- Niche partitioning: Species living together use resources slightly differently, enabling them to coexist in that community
