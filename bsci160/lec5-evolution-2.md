# Evidence For Evolution Via Natural Selection

## Introduction
- Components of proof of evolution via natural selection include:
    - Direct observation (from artificial selection)
    - Fossils (via radiometric data)
    - Homologous structures ( structures with similar underlying features but different uses), showing divergent evolution
        - Analogous structures, on the other hand, show convergent evolution
    - Vestigial structures (e.g. eye sockets in cave animals, goosebumps)
    - Biogeography (study of distribution of organisms)
    - DNA/mutational similarities
    - Gemetic drift: Random changes in allele frequency
        - Bottleneck event: Event that kills a large number of individuals
        - Founder effect: When small number of individuals fiund a new colony

## Alleles
- Fixed allele: When an allele is the only one present
- Lost allele: WHen an allele is absent from all living individuals
- If drift is stronger than selection, allele frequencies will fluctuate randomy
- If selection is stronger than drift, allele frequencies will fluctuate while moving toward the more "beneficial" trait being prevalent

## Sexual selection

### Non-random mating
- Can be adaptive
- Can be caused by physical location (and lead to interbreeding)
- Two parents give rise to genetically unique offspring through the fusion of gametes that occurs via meiosis
    - The fusion of gametes is called syngamy
- While there is some randomness involved, choice of partners is not random

### Asexual reproduction
- One individual gives rise to *genetically identical* offspring
- No change in chromosome number
- All prokaryotes (bacteria/archaea) reproduce asexually

### Eukaryotic reproduction
- Some eukaryotes are facultative sexual reproducers, meaning that they switch back and forth between sexual and asexual reproduction
    - Example: Daphnia spp. are asexual when conditions are good, and sexual during times of stress
    - Example: Stickbugs, which switch based on the abundance of males
- Few animals are obligate asexual reproducers
    - Example: Some whiptail lizards
    - Asexual species tend not to persist
- Over 95% of plants and animals can or always do reproduce sexually
