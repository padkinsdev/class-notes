# Global Abiotic Factors

## Biomes
- Deserts:
    - Deserts get little to no precipitation on a yearly basis
    - Deserts also tend to have massive temperature fluctuations across the year and even across a single day
- Tundra
    - Tundras tend to be similarly dry and extremely cold
    - Most soil is frozen year-round (permafrost). During summer, the top layer of soil thaws but the rest remains frozen
    - Tundras are sometimes considered deserts

## Factors

### Hemispheres
- The angle at which sunlight hits the earth in a region is dependent on the latitude of that region
    - A 0 degree latitude means that sunlight hits at a 90 degree angle
- The tilt of the Earth's axis of rotation causes seasons (e.g. winter occurs in the Northern Hemisphere when that hemisphere is facing the Sun less directly than the Southern Hemisphere)
    - Migratory birds often track summer conditions by flying to whichever hemisphere is currently experiencing summer
    - Temperature and day length is more consistent near the equator because the tilt of the Earth's axis has less effect

### Wind and water
- Hadley cells:
    - Collisions of air currents causes air to rise. This causes air to cool, and the cooling of air causes precipitation to fall. Warmer air holds more water vapor
- Temperature decreases with altitude/elevation because the atmosphere gets thinner and farther away from the warm surface of the earth
- There are many "circulation cells" across the globe caused by major air circulation patterns
- The Coriolis effect explains patterns of prevailing surface winds (Earth rotates faster at the equator)
- Global circulation of surface water (e.g. oceans) also modifies climate patterns on land
- Rain shadows occur when air rises over one side of a mountain and cools, resulting in rainfall. The opposite side of the mountain is considered to be in a rain shadow, and they tend to be quite barren
- Temperature influences oxygen content
    - As the temperature of a body of water (e.g. a sea) drops, the amount of dissolved oxygen also decreases

### Soil and the ground
- Warm, humid environments with more rain have more acidic soil
- pH of soil also depends on parent material (rock)
    - Soils are classified by the amount of sand, silt, and clay
- Underground and aquatic habitats are buffered from terrestrial changes, and thus don't experience the same temperature fluctuations that shallow soil does
