# Plant Reproduction

##

## Adaptive radiation
- A population of one species dispersing through an area and each finding a distinct role/food source/habitat
- There have been four types of successful adaptive radiations of land plants
    - Nonvascual plants/Bryophytes
        - Mosses
        - Liverworts
        - Hornworts
    - Ferns and relatives
    - Angiosperms (flowering plants)
    - Gymnosperms

### Nonvascular plants
- Lack of vascularity means:
    - Lack of structural support and water transport (despite reliance on water for reproduction)
    - Limited dispersal
- Sporophytes and gametophytes are similar in size
- Moss, for example, consists of single strands of individual gametophytes
- The sporophyte with grow out of the gametophyte only during the reproductive stage
- Male gametophytes release sperm with tails
    - Swimming sperm require water to swim to a female gametophyte with an egg
- The plants spends most of its time in the gametophyte stage

### Seedless vascular plants
- Examples:
    - Horsetails
    - Ferns
    - Lycophytes (club mosses)
- Ferns can grow quite large, and formed entire forests during the paleozoic era
- Principle breakthrough was vascular tissue, allowing for large size due to better structural support against gravity, and efficient distribution of water and nutrients via xylem and phloem
- Seedless vascular plants represent the development of "true leaves", being flat surfaces for photosynthesis
- Life cycle:
    - Swimming sperm require water to swim to a female gametophyte with an egg
    - In contrat to moss, the gametophyte is short-lived, and the plant spends most of its time in the sporophyte stage
    - Dispersal occurs via explosion of the spores to facilitate dispersal

### Gymnosperm
- Gymnosperms lack ovaries for development of seeds (hence the meaning of the term gymnosperm, "naked seed")
- Gymnosperms were once the dominant for of land plant
- Less than 1200 species remain
- Gymnosperms exhibit two main breakthroughs:
    - Evolution of seeds (an embryo protected and packed in with a food supply). Seeds allow for dispersal via wind and animals, as well as the ability to wait for favorable conditions to germinate and grow
    - Use of pollen, i.e. sperm with no tails that can be carried by the wind
- Male cones make pollen which contains sperm, female cones make ovules which contain eggs. After fertilization and maturity, seeds are released
- The tree is the sporophyte life stage
- Wind-borne dispersal of pollen requires that large amounts of pollen are produced, which is somewhat inefficient

### Angiosperms (flowering plants)
- Angiosperms are incredibly successful, with more than 250k soecies alive today
- Despite appearing 130 million years ago, angiosperms have demonstrated rapid diversification
- Two main types:
    - Flowers, which use animals to pollinate. This allows for more efficient pollination due to the lack of reliance on wind, as well as mitigating inbreeding and facilitating reproduction even in low abundance
    - Fruits, which are typically derived from the ovary of the flower
- Life cycle:
    - Like gymnosperms, spend most of their time as diploid sporophytes and use sperm with no tail
    - Create flowers to attract pollinators, and fruits to attract dispersal agents
