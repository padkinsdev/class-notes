# Speciation

## Species splitting
- Occasionally and ancestor species will split into two descendant species through the following processes:
    - Allopatric speciation: Physical barrier divides population
    - Sympatric speciation: No physical barrier separating the population
    - Peripatric speciation: Subcase of allopatric speciation in which a subset of a population splits off to an isolated area (geographically)
- For speciation to occur there must be genetic divergence among populations over time, and eventually reproductive isolation
- Adaptive radiation occurs when individuals of a population spread out across a geographic area, finding different food sources, roles, and habitats. This can be the result of sympatric or allopatric speciation

## Reproductive isolation
- Reproductive isolation can occur due to the following reasons:
    - Prezygotic barriers, in which formation of a zygote is prevented
    - Postzygotic barriers, in which zygotes are unable to become viable offspring

### Prezygotic barriers
- Habitat Isolation: Many individuals occupy the same range and may be able to hybridize, but prefer different habitats so mating does not occur
- Temporal Isolation: Individuals can interbreed, but are capable of reproduction at different times
- Behavioral Isolation: Individuals are capable of interbreeding, but do not do so due to differences in courtship or other behavior
- Mechanical Isolation: Morphological differences prevent successful mating
- Gametic Isolation: Gametes do not recognize each other due to different receptors

### Postzygotic barriers
- Reduced hybrid viability: Hybrid offspring do not develop or have reduced survivability
- Hybrid infertility: Hybrids can survive but not reproduce (often due to differences in chromosome number)
- Hybrid breakdown: First generation hybrids are reproductively viable, but second generation ones are sterile or weak
