# Sex and Sexual Selection

## Sex

### Why is sex so common?
- Tangled bank hypothesis: Sexual reproduction is advantageous in complex environments with limited resources
    - Sex provides genetic variability in offspring
    - Variability promotes differentiation
    - Differentiation increases chances of survival
    - Corollary: Stable, predictable environments are conducive to asexual reproduction, whereas unstable environments are conducive to sexual reproduction
- Red Queen Hypothesis:
    - Sex produces varied offspring
    - Allows for rapid evolution, and thus lets species keep up in the evolutionary arms race
- Sexual reproduction (between unrelated individuals) can lower the frequency of deleterious alleles
    - Breeding with unrelated individuals brings in good alleles to mask bad ones

### Why do some organisms engage in asexual reproduction?
- Sex is inefficient, requiring twice the number of adults to produce a given number of offspring
    - It's better to just produce all females, since females are the ones actually producing the offspring
- Finding and courting a mate is inefficient, energetically costly, and risky for survival
    - Courtship involves finding mates, producing and maintaining sexual displays, and caring for young
    - STDs, fights, etc. are all a byproduct of sexual reproduction, and can be harmful to survival

## Sexual selection

### Non-random vs. random mating
- In non-random mating, mates are chosen/limited, caused by physical preferences, physical barriers, and low population size
- Sexual selection is a form of natural selection that results from differential success among individuals in acquiring mates
    - Notably, sexual selection can operate against survival instincts

### Differential investment
- Sperm require less energy to produce, and thus males have a smaller stake in sexual reproduction
    - Male reproductive success is determined by number of times they can mate
    - Males need not stick around after mating
- Eggs require more energy to reproduce, and thus females have a larger stake in sexual reproduction
    - Female reproductive success is determined by the number of eggs they can produce and nurture, not the number of times they can mate
- Effectively males prioritize quantity, females prioritize quality
- Consequences:
    - Males will compete for opportunities to mate, whereas females will be selective in mating
- Sexual dimorphism is the difference between males and females of a species
    - Broadly speaking, males tend to be more flashy (larger weapons/musculature, greater tendency to vocalize and be aggressive) and physically equipped for combat (per Darwin)
    - Sometimes being more overtly noticeable/flashy can be detrimental to survival due to increased attention from predators
- Male competition and female choice act as selective factors

### Male-male competition
- Competition between males is a form of intrasexual selection
- Male-male combat
    - Often males will physically fight one another for the opportunity to mate, with the winner gaining access to females
- An alternative to being flashy is being *sneaky*, where males look like females
- Sperm competiton
    - Females often mate repeatedly with multiple males, with male success being whose sperm gets to the female's egg first

### Female choice
- Female mating preferences are widely recognized as being responsible for the rapid and divergent evolution of male secondary sexual traits
- Assortative mating:
    - Positive assortative mating: Individuals choose mates with traits simialr to their own (more common)
    - Negative assortative mating: Individuals choose mates with traits dissimilar from their own
- Direct benefits:
    - Nuptual gifts (e.g. prey) given by males to females
- Sexual cannibalism:
    - Female mantids will sometimes consume males after mating
    - Hypothesis: Males that are eaten may end up producing more offspring than those that survive
- Good genes hypothesis:
    - Ability to produce and maintain elaborate ornamentation indicates good genes (i.e. can afford to take risks and waste energy)
    - May indicate resistance to parasites or developmental stress
    - Only works if the indicator is honest!
- Fisher's sexy son hypothesis (runaway selection):
    - Prerequisite is initial, genetically based, female preference
    - Males inherent their fathers' attractive traits, and females inherit preference for those traits
    - Those males are sexually successful, and the cycle continues
    - Ultimately the traits could become so exaggerated that they inhibit functioning
