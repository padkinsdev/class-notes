# Population Ecology, Continued

## Demography and reproduction

- A life table is an age-specific summary of the survival pattern of a population
- Survivorship takes three trends:
  - Type 1: High survivorship over most of the lifespan that drops rapidly at the end of the lifespan
  - Type 2: Steady survivorship across the lifespan
  - Type 3: Rapid initial decline in survivorship that flattens out as an organism gets older
- Trade-offs: Organisms must split energy across survival, reproduction, and growth
- Frequency of reproduction:
  - Semelparity: Single reproduction that produces a large number of offspring
  - Iteroparity: Repeated reproduction after a certain age, with each incidence of reproduction producing a small number of offspring
- Environments exist on two approximate axes: stable/unstable and predictable/unpredictable
- Reproductive strategies can be categorized as:
  - **r-selected**: Prioritize high reproductive rates
  - **k-selected**: Often prioritize quality over quantity due to being near carrying capacity (K)
- Bet-hedging refers to a strategy for highly variable (unstable and unpredictable) environments. It refers to making a large variety of offspring to try to maximize the number that survive
