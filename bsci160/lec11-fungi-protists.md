# Fungi and Protists

## Eukaryotes
- Eukaryotes are characterized by the following:
    - Having a nucleus
    - Performing meiosis
    - Having organelles
    - All have mitochondria
    - Some have chloroplasts
    - Some are multicellular
    - Some have a cell wall
- 8 major clades of eukaryotes exist:
    - Alveolates (considered protists)
    - Stramenopiles (considered protists)
    - Rhizarians (considered protists)
    - Excavates (considered protists)
    - Plantae (plants): Photosynthetic autotrophs with cell walls made of cellulose
    - Amoebozoans (considered protists)
    - Fungi: Absorptive heterotrophs within cell walls made of chitin
    - Animals: Ingestive heterotrophs with no cell walls

### Protists
- Remember: All eukaryotes that are not fungi, plants, or animals are considered protists
    - Protists are not, however, all more closely related to one another than they are to plants/animals/fungi. In other words they are a polyphyletic group
    - The "protist" grouping is fairly useless, but does function as a catch-all term of exclusion from the three aforementioned clades
- Protists are primarily unicellular
- Protists primarily have haploid life cycles, though some variation does exist
- Some protists eat organic matter which is digested by lysosomes then excreted by exocytic vesicles (heterotrophism), and some perform photosynthesis (autotrophism)
- Prominent examples of protists:
    - Helpful:
        - Phytoplankton, which are a valuable food source in aquatic anvironments
        - Half of all coral coexists with dinoflagellates, which are photosynthetic zooxanthellae
        - Brown algae comprise the base of kelp forest ecosystems
    - Harmful:
        - Diatoms and dinoflagellates, while not inherently harmful, can reproduce in enormous numbers when conditions are favorable (algal bloom). The toxins they produce can kill or harm vertebrates including fish and humans
        - Plasmodium causes malaria
        - Giardia lambblia causes diarrhea
        - Trichomonis vginalis is a human STD
        - Trypanosoma cause Chagas disease/African sleeping sickness
- Main takeaways:
    - Primarily unicellular
    - Not a monophyletic group
    - Diverse and ecologically important
    - Diversification happened a long time ago

### Fungi
- Important components of natural ecosystems
- Penicillin originated from a fungus, and they are a source of food (e.g. beer, bread, blue cheese)
- Some fungi are edible, others are incredibly toxic. Similarly, some fungi are extremely small whereas others are quite large
- Surprisingly, fungi are more closely related to animals than plants
- A prominent type of fungi are yeast, which are notably not monophyletic
- Fungi grow using hyphae, or multicellular branching growth

### Diet
- Fungi are absorptive heterotrophs, meaning that they eat organic material by absorbing it and digesting with enzymes
- **There are no photosynthetic fungi**
- Fungi function as decomposers, feeding on dead material

### Ecology
- Pathogenicity:
    - Spruce Bloom rust is caused by Chrysomyxa arctostaphyli
    - Candida fungi cause yeast infections
- Close relationships:
    - Mychorrhizae: Exchange nutrients for sugar with plants
    - Lichens: Symbiotic association between fungi and algae
- It is unknown what the total number of fungal species is, and some are found in odd places such as deep sea thermal vents

### Reproduction
- Fungi life cycles include both asexual and sexual cycles
- In some cases the sexual stage can look sufficiently different from the asexual one to appear to be a different species
