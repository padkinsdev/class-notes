# Ecology

## Introduction

- Ecology is the interaction of prganisms with each other and with their environment
- Organismal ecology is the interaction of individuals with their environment
- A population is a group of individuals of the same species living in the same environment
- Communities are interactions among different species in a shared environment
- Ecosystems are interactions between communities and their environments
- The biosphere is the influence of energy on organisms across the planet

### Humans in ecology

- Ecology of non-human species impacts human health!
  - Zoonosis (spillover of disease from non-human sources)
  - The human gut is a microbiome consisting of many bacterial, viral, and fungal species
- Human activities are altering non-human ecology in the form of habitat destruction, pollution, overharvesting, climate change, etc.
- Ecology in practice can consist of
  - Conservation ecology: Understanding and preventing extinction of vulnerable species
  - Restorative ecology: Restoring the health of damaged ecosystems

### Biotic vs. abiotic factors

- Abiotic factors are the non-living physical and chemical parameters of an environment
  - Adaptation: Evolutionary processes over generations which result in traits that increase survival and reproduction in an environment
  - Acclimation: Short-term reversible adjustment of physiology/behavior to handle environmental stressors
- Biotic factors are the libing components of an evironment

### Generalists vs. specialists

- Generalists are adaptive to a range of environments, and have an advantage in changing conditions
- Specialists are suited for specific environments, and have an advantage when conditions remain constant

## Biomes

- Abiotic factors can strongly influence the nature of ecosystems and the species that inhabit them, producing *biomes*
- A biome is a large geographic region characterized by a specific set of environmental conditions
  - This includes the biological community that forms in response to the physical environment
  - Unlike ecosystems, biomes can have geographically disjoint (separate) regions, as long as all of the regions have similar biotic and abiotic factors

### Aquatic biomes

- The vast majority of water is in the ocean (saline water)
  - Rivers and streams compose 0.0002% of the earth's water
  - Bodies of standing or still freshwater (ponds and lakes) compose 0.007% of the Earth's water
- Marine ecosystems, which are 96.5% of the Earth's water, can be broken down into:
  - Estuaries: Where freshwater of a river meets saltwater of the ocean
  - Intertidal zones: The area between the high tide line and the low tide line
  - Neridic zones: The area over the low tide line which extends over the continental shelf
    - Sunlight passes through this zone, so photosynthesis can still occur
  - The open ocean, which is composed of two zones:
    - Surface zone: The first few hundred meters deep of the ocean (where light penetrates)
    - Deep zone: Below the surface zone (light does not penetrate here)

### Land biomes

- Land biomes are mostly determined by their climate, and are often classified by dryness and temperature over space and time (two axes)
  - Tropical forests are found in regions near the equator and remaid warm and humid year-round
  - Temperate deciduous forests are forests with temperate conditons consisting of trees that lose their leaves in the fall
  - Boreal forests are typically dominated by conifers and tend to have low temperatures on an annual basis
  - Grasslands (praries) are mostly populated by grasses and other non-woody plants. They tend to get minimal rain
  - The chaparral (shrubland) has mild, wet winters and hot, dry summers
  - Deserts are areas that receive less than 25 cm of rain per year
