# Proteins

- Enzymes potentiate reactions, with an active site that substrates bind to
- Different cell types arise from differential gene expression
- Types of mutations:
    - Silent mutation: No change in amino acid sequence (usually a point mutation, meaning single nucleotide)
    - Missense mutation: Change in an amino acid, variable effect size
    - Nonsense mutation: Change in an amino acid which causes termination of chain
- To be considered a single nucleotide polypmorphism (SNP), a mutation must occur in at least 1% of the population
    - SNPs are often used to determine ancestry. These are called *ancestry informative markers*
- Insertions and deletions of nucleotides can cause frameshift mutations, meaning that the codons are altered
- Some insertions/deletions of repeated elements are called short tandem repeats (STRs)
    - Most common STRs are 3-7 base pairs in length
    - These vary in copy number across individuals and are the gold standard for forensic DNA fingerprinting
- Chromosomal mutations alter large chunks of genes
    - This includes deletions, insertions, translocations (transfer from one chromosome to another), and inversions (a section of a chromosome is inverted)
    - Chromosomal deletions tend to be the most impactful of these mutations
