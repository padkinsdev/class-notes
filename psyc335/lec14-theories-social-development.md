# Theories of social development

## Psychoanalytic theories
- Main theorists: Freud and Erikson

### Freud
- Freud believed that all behavior is driven by biological drives centered on erogenous zones
- Stages of psychosexual development:
    - Oral (first year): The mother is the strongest love-object, and oral activities are the greatest source of satisfaction and pleasure. Complications lead to nail biting, chewing, smoking, overeating, etc.
    - Anal (1-3 years): The primary source of pleasure comes from defecation. Complications lead to obsessively clean or sloppy personalities/fixations
    - Phallic (3-6 years): Pleasure is localized in the genitalia. COmplications lead to anxiety about sex and narcissism
    - Latency (6-12 years): Characterized by the channeling of sexual energy into socially acceptable activities. Complications lead to problems in school/social life
    - Sexual maturation is complete and sexual intercourse becomes a major goal
- 3 instinctual drives:
    - Id: Innate biological drives
    - Ego: Reality. Mediates the id and superego
    - Superego: Morality and conscience
- Freud's theories are difficult to test empirically. Additionally, he didn't actually work with children.

### Erikson
- Accepted basic Freudian tenets, but incorporated social aspects
- Theorized about 8 different "developmental crises" that one must navigate

## Learning theories
- Primary theorists: Watson, Skinner, Bandura
- Emphasize the role of external factors like reward and punishment
- Bandura emphasized the role of the active child
- Behaviorists emphasized continuity in mechanisms of change alongside individual differences in response to rewards and punishments

### Bandura's social learning theory
- Development is driven by social learning, notably vicarious reinforcement (seeing someone else receive a reward or punishment)
- Bandura's theories are based on empirical research with easily testable principles. However, they ignored the active role of the child as well as predisposition to aggression

## Theories of social cognition
- Focus on the child's ability to reason about their own and other people's feelings, motives, and behaviors
- Emphasis on self-socialization and active choices a chald makes in their development

### Dodge's social information processing theory
- Motivated by studies of variation in childhood aggression
- **Hostile attribution bias**: Assumption that others are inherently antagonistic or hold hostile intentions toward oneself
- 6 steps in solving social problems:
    - Encoding of the problematic event
    - Interpret social cues
    - Formulate a goal
    - Generate strategies
    - Evaluate potential strategies
    - Enact behavior
- The link between hostile attribution bias and aggression becomes cyclical over time
- Strengths: Based on empirical research, highlight subjective interpretation and cognition
- Weaknesses: Ignore biological factors

## Ecological theories
- Bronfenbrenner's bioecological model
- Emphasize sociocultural context
- A child's environment is composed of a series of nested structures, and each level impacts development
- Systems:
    - Microsystem: Immediate surroundings
    - Mesosystem: Interaction between microsystems
    - Exosystem: External environment
    - Macrosystem: Cultural context
    - Chronosystem: Time-based changes
- Takes a broader approach to development, but de-emphasizes biology
