# Risk and Resilience

### Resilience
- Resilience is the ability of a dynamic system ro adapt successfully to disturbances/disruptions
- Key factors: disturbances (risk factors) and adaptation (positive outcomes)
- Developmental stage and cultural context impact relevant/expected outcomes
- Immigrant communities undergo the acculturation process, meaning they adapt in response to intercultural conflict
- Concepts (Equifinality/Multifinality):
    1. Equifinality: Many predictors lead to a single outcome
    2. Multifinality: A single predictor can produce multiple outcomes
- How do people adapt to adversity?
    1. Drawing on resources, e.g. familial/community resources
- Properties of resilience:
    1. The capacity of an individual to adapt to adversity
    2. A process of drawing on resources
    3. Varies based on examined outcome domain
    4. Determined by risk factors, positive outcomes, and resilience factors
    5. Developmentally, culturally, and contextually specific
    6. Not static
- Risk and resilience are studied using regression across a quantified sample (as is the case with most quantified data in research contexts)
- Obviously, regression does not indicate a causative effect, as confounding variables (biological, behavioral, environemtal) may exist
- Resilience factors can be promotive or protective. Protective factors show an increasing factor at higher levels of risk. Promotive factors promote adaptation at all levels of risk
- Some factors can be both promotive and protective, depending on whether the relationship is statistically significant
- Diathesis-stree interaction: Some individuals are more vulnerable to the negative impacts of adverse experiences
    * E.g. phenylketonuria (inability to metabolize phenylalanine)
- Vantage-sensitivity interactions: Some individuals reap more benefits from positive environmental experiences
- Differential susceptibility interactions: SOme individuals who are vulnerable to negative might *also* reap more benefits from promotive esperiences. In other words, more to lose but more to gain. Could be moderated by stress responses, reward processing, neuroticism, extraversion, etc.
- Socioeconomic resources include income, education, and occupation
- Socioeconomic status tends to persist across generations, and is associated with maladaptive outcomes across the life course. However, moderating/mitigating effects do exist
- Broadly speaking, access to socioeconomic resources in childhood results in higher earned income as an adult
