# Cognitive Ability and Academic Opportunity

## Theories of intelligence

### g (general intelligence)
- An ability that is common to all intellectual tasks
- Domain-general

### Basic abilities theories
- Crystallized and fluid intelligence theory: Crystallized memory is fact-knowing and fluid intelligence is problem solving and attention capacity
- Seven primary mental abilities theory: Word fluency, spatial visualization, numbering, rote memory, verbal meaning, perceptual speed, reasoning
- Somewhere between domain-general and domain-specific

### Multiple processes theories
- Instead of abilities, these theories focus on which processes are needed for taking in, transforming, and applying information
- Strongly in line with Information Processing Theory and very mechanism-specific

### Three-stratum model of intelligence
- An integrated model of intelligence which is the primary model used today
- Breaks down general intelligence into two lower strata of skills/domains

### Gardner's theory of Multiple Intelligences
- 8 different forms of intelligence: linguistic, logical-mathematical, spatial, musical, naturalistic, bodily-kinesthetic, intrapersonal, interpersonal
- EMphasizes that competence requires both inherent (genetic) predisposition *and* sociocultural nurtuting
- Culture determines which types of intelligence are valued over others
- Competence in one area does not imply competence in another

### Sternberg's theory of Successful Intelligence
- "The ability to achieve success in life, given one's personal standards, within one's sociocultural context"
- Intelligence cannot be assessed outside of sociocultural context
- Success is composed of three ability domains:
    - Analytic (linguistic, math, spatial skills)
    - Practical (everyday reasoning and conflict resolution)
    - Creative

## Measuring intelligence
- Originally developed by Binet in France in response to the need for universal public education
- The first intelligence test was a test to assess school performance
- The current gold standard for intelligence measurement is the Wechsler Intelligence Scales for Children (WISC), which is most consistent with the Carroll Three-Stratum Theory of Intelligence
- The WISC produces an overall score and subscores for verbal comprehension, visual-spatial processing, working memory, etc.
- The overall intelligence score is called the intelligence quotient (IQ) and is standardized to a Gaussian distribution relative to the age of the test taker

## Controversies in IQ testing
- Mean differences in test scores are due to artifacts in the testing process rather than true differences (e.g. prevalence of imagery in a culture)
- IQ testing is not consistent
- Contextual factors such as mindsets and school quality impact IQ scores
- Reducing a person's intelligence to a single number is very reductive
- One's belief in their ability to influence their intelligence affects their IQ scoring. Thus, education about growth mindsets is beneficial for academic achievement and IQ scoring

## Context in intelligence
- Making reasoning (e.g. mathematical) contextually relevant to the child improves performance
- Socioeconomic status is incredibly impactful to the heritability of IQ, with high SES corresponding to high rates of IQ heritability and low SES corresponding to greater variation in IQ heritability

## Mapping IQ to academic performance
- Reading requires mastery of phonemic awareness, word identification, vocabulary, and comprehension. Additionally, reading requires motivation and exposure
- Writing requires strategy and narrative structure formation as well as metacognitive understanding (understanding that readers may not posess the same knowledge as the writer)
- Math largely relies on automatization through retrieval and repetition. It also relies on numerical magnitude representations (understanding of quantities as greater or smaller than others)

### Achievement gap
- The disparity in academic performance between groups of students. This manifests through grades, standardized test scores, course selection, dropout rates, and college-completion rates
- The achievement gap is documented by racial-ethnic identity, socioeconomic resources, English language proficiency, and sex/gender identity
- Not all states and school districts are equally successful at minimizing the achievement gap

### Academic opportunity
- Intergenerational wealth, structural racism, and systematic disadvantage of marginalized means that school quality varies
- The underlying question is one of equality vs equity
