# Identity

## Self-concept
- Self-concept becomes increasingly more complex with age
    - Based on internal and external evaluations
- Self-esteem: One's overall evaluation of the worth of the self and the feelings one has about that evaluation
    - Emerges around 8 years of age
    - Has a cultural component related to whether a culture is individualistic or collectivistic
- Erikson theorized that all adolescents experience an identity crisis as a means of separating from their parents. This culminates in identity achievement, which represents a successful resolution
    - Resolution occurs through exploration, which is an idea that many theories of identity hold in common
- Marcia theorized four groups related to identity exploration along two axes: commitment and exploration
    - Moratorium status relates to high exploration and low commitment

## Ethnic-racial identity
- Ethnic-racial identity: A dynamic developmental process by which individuals explore, internalize, and make meaning about their ethnic-racial group membership

### Helm's model of ethnic identity development
- 4 stages:
    - Pre-encounter
    - Encounter: A prejudicial encounter
    - Immersion/emersion: Withdrawing into one's culture
    - Internalization/commitment: Integrate personal and culture identity/enact new identity

### Phinney's three stage model of ethnic identity
- Unexamined ethnic identity
- Ethnic identity search/moratorium
- Ethnic identity achievement

## Racial identity in childhood
- Based on Clark and Clark's doll study, even children have a concept of race and internalize racial prejudices
- At birth children do not seem to differentiate based on race
- At 3 months children begin to differentiate based on skin tone and physical features
- 6 months: Infants begin to rely on social cues of own-race individuals compared with other-race individuals
- 24 months: Children identify their own race with low accuracy
- 36 months: Children consistently choose same-race playmates
- 5 years: Children of all ages expect white children to have better posessions
- 6 years: Black children assign a better status to traditionally white jobs
- Preschoolers do not understand the significance of being in an ethnic group but can identify the ethnic group they belong to and perform behaviors related to this identity
- High resolution: Feeling personally responsible for addressing needs of those within the community
- Adolescents with low commitment (ethnic-racial identity affirmation) tend to show greater externalizing behaviors in the face of discrimination and violence
- Higher ERI affirmation is linked to fewer depressive symptoms through the perceived ability to cope with problems
    - Essentially, higher ethnic-racial identity (ERI) affirmation leads to greater coping behavior and thus less depressive symptoms
- Boosting ERI involves engagement in cultural customs as well as cultural socialization. This influences the child's view of of the group and thus their indentity exploration and commitment
    - Racial socialization has been identified as a protective factor
    - Cultural socialization
    - Preparation for bias
    - Egalitarianism
    - Promotion of mistrust of interracial communications
