# Learning

## Prelinguistic communication
- Parents:
    - Infant-directed speech ("parentese")
    - Enforcement of turn-taking in conversation
    - Engagement and support of join attention
- Children:
    - Use social signals (emotive expression) and gesturing
    - Intentionally attempt to communicate

## Perceiving the sounds of language
- Phonemes: basic units of sound in speech that build words with statistical regularity
- Prosody: pitch, loudness, rhythm (melodic aspects of language)
- The physical difference between similar sounds can be described in terms of a physically continuous dimension
- Young infants can distinguish well between phonemes used in both their native and non-native languages (6-8 mont old)
- Older infants (10-12 month old) onward struggle to differentiate between phonemes that are not used in their native language. As such, early childhood language exposure shapes how speech is perceived through a process of perceptual narrowing
- To facilitate an infant's word learning, their caregiver provides speech. Correspondingly, the child uses statistical learning and an understanding of pragmatic cues to interpret the language
- Quality of speech is better than quantity for children
- Family Investment model: Parents with higher income have access to greater resources to enrich their child
