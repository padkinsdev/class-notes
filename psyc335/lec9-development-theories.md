# Developmental Theories

## Piaget
- Piaget viewed children as constructivists, meaning they are active, intrinsically motivated to learn, and learn many lessons independently
- **Assimilation**: Integrating reality into one's own views/concepts
- **Acommodation**: Altering reality to acommodate existing beliefs/concepts
- A healthy mix of acommodation and assimilation produces equilibration, i.e. a newfound understanding and appreciation for the world
- Distinct, hierarchical stages of development occur

### Piaget's stages of development

#### Sensorimotor
- Children explore and experience to the extent possible. 
- More simply, they touch everything and put it in their mouths. 
- Higher order cognition has not developed yet, so scolding a baby is generally ineffective. 
- However, children in this stage can still for negative associations
- Object permanence develops from this stage, hence why parents play hide and seek with their children in this stage
- **A-not-B error**: Looking for an object in the last place it was found rather the place where it was observed to be hidden. One outcome of the sensorimotor stage is that children no longer exhibit this behavior
- **Deferred imitation**: Repetition of observed behavior days after it was observed

#### Pre-operational phase
- Children develop symbolic representations for objects
- Theory of mind begins to develop and children are egocentric during this phase
- **Centration**: The tendency to focus on only one aspect of an object rather than multiple simultaneous ones
- Children struggle to perform conservation tasks, such as conservation of mass or number

#### Concrete operational
- Logical thinking
- Perspective taking is more developed and children are less egocentric
- Children may struggle with more complex logical tasks that involve simulataneous, *systematic* testing of multiple dimensions

#### Formal operational
- Children can perform systematic experiments and can think abstractly about things like morality

### Critiques of Piaget
- Infant and child capabilities are underestimated
- Impact of culture and environment are underestimated
- His model is vague regarding actual underlying mechanisms
- Thinking and development is not as discrete as he claims

## Information-processing theory
- Emphasis on thinking as a process across time
- Considers cognitive development as information processing, e.g. in terms of task analysis and temporal sequence of mental operations
- Children are considered problem solvers within this theory
- Cognitive development stems from children's gradual ability to overcome cognitive ("software") limitations to problem solving
- **Working memory**: Memory used when actively processing/maintaining/processing information. Presumed to be due to myelination and stronger neural signals
- **Long-term memory**: Accumulated knowledge
- **Executive function**: Inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, and selective attention
- There is evidence for a continuous acceleration of processing as a child grows older
- Certain strategies emerge between ages 5-8:
    * **Rehearsal**: Repeating information to memorize it
    * Selective attention

## Core-knowledge theories
- Children possess prior knowledge due to evolutionary processes
- Children do learn, but this is facilitated by the existence of core knowledge that humans inherently possess
- Face processing: Very young children inherently prefer faces over other objects
- Language: Children quickly grasp grammatical rules
- Infants can discriminate between differences in magnituded and quantity
- Deception: Children may inherently understand the need to please others, leading to lying
- Development consists of both domain-specific and domain-general processes

## Sociocultural theories
- Primarily know for Vygotsky's work
- Learning is driven by social interations
- Emphasis on guided participation and social subjectivity
- Vygotsky proposed a model of the zone of proximal development, where there are things children can do independently, things they can do with help, and things they can't do. The zone of proximal development is the second of the three
- Social and cultural tools appear in all societies
- Inclination towards trust for others is natural

## Dynamic systems theory
- Focused on mechanisms, continuity, and discontinuity in development
- Essentially integrating across domains (motor + cognitive, etc.)
- Thought shapes action and action shapes thought
- Training children to advance their actions improves performance on related tasks/actions
- Emphasize the collaboration between different systems to create the child as a whole being
- Example: Active games (Xbox Kinect, Wii Fit)
- A *lot* of focus on movement
