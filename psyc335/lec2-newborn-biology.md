# Newborn Biology

### Infant Mortality
- Infant mortality has decreased over the last millennium or so
- The US has higher rates of infant mortality than many other industrialized countries, driven in part by racial/ethnic disparities in such mortality
- Such disparities persist even after controlling for income and education, and are perhaps mediated instead by disparities in toxic stress and midical bias during birth
- Low birth weight (< 5.5 pounds) and premature birth (< 35 weeks) are additional risk factors for infant mortality
- On average, low birth weight babies experience more medical complications, developmental difficulties, and challenges. This relationship is confounded by socioeconomic status

### Newborn capabilities

#### Reflexes
- Newborns have a number of reflexes, some of which disappear or become voluntary. Retention of certain reflexes may indicate developmental challenges
- Blinking and breathing are examples of such reflexes

#### Sleep
- Newborns sleep twice as much as young adults, with sleep patterns changing dramatically with age
- Newborns spend ~50% of their time in REM sleep. One theory, called the autostimulation theory, suggests that this REM sleep makes up for a lack of prior external stimulation. Other theories include learning and sensorimotor calibration
- Newborns don't have an established sleep-wake cycle, and need to be fed every 2-3 hours
- Beginning around 3 months, caregivers can begin "sleep training" if desired

#### Crying
- Crying begins as an expression of distress, then evolves to become communicative of the infant's need
- A colicky baby is one that cries persistently for no apparent reason, and is estiamted to affect ~20% of infants worldwide
- Soothing techniques for crying babies include distraction, pacifiers, rocking, etc., but only swaddling is empirically proven to soothe a crying baby. This is believed to be due to mimicry of conditions in the womb (tactile stinulation, warmth, etc.)
- Some debate exists on whether letting a baby cry freely or "cry it out" is detrimental to infant development. Some evidence suggests that doing so leads to development of self-soothing behavior. 
- There is some concern about caregiver distress resulting from infant crying, with some imaging research indicating increased activation of audio processing and empathy mediating sections of the brain when a parent hears their own infant cry vs. another parent's infant
