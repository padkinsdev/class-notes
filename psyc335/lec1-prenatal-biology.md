# Prenatal Biological Development

### Gametes
- Gametes consist of eggs (oocytes) and sperm.
- Spermatogenesis occurs accross the entire lifespan for males, whereas primary oogenesis occurs at birth for females, and resumes at the onset of menstruation, ceasing at the onset of menopause.
- During fertilization in humans, sperm travels through the vaginal canal into the uterus. A single sperm cell enters the egg, and the egg embeds itself into the uterine wall to receive nutrients from the mother's body.

### Likelihood of conception
- Sexual intercourse must be precisely timed for fertilization to be successful.
- Sperm can only live for 5 dayswithin the uterus, and ovum die or dissolve within 12-24 hours if not fertilized.

### Sexual determination
- Sex consists of genetic sex (xx/xy), gonadal sex (testis/ovaries), and phenotypic sex.
- In prenatal development, the presence or absence of androgens causes the development of male or female genitalia, respectively.
- Phenotypic sex consists of primary and secondary sex characteristics, e.g. fallopian tubes, vagina, penis, breasts, etc.

### Prenatal biological development
- After fertilization, the ovum begins to divide, and cell differentiation. Selective apoptosis (cell death) also occurs.
- Cell division occurs from head to toe, referred to as cephalocaudal development.
- Support systems for prenatal development include placenta, umbilical cord, and the amniotic sac. These provide nutrients and filter toxins.
- The prenatal (gestational) period is 40 weeks, which despite pupular belief, is close to 10 months.

### Gestation
- Gestation consists of 3 periods: germinal, embryonic, and fetal.
- Germinal development (weeks 0-4) consists of blastocyst development into the placenta and embryo.
- Embryonic development (weeks 4 to 9) consists of development of all major organs, with the heart beginning to beat at 6 weeks
- Fetal development (weeks 10-40) consists of lower body growth and continued development of the brain and spinal cord

### Fetal behavior
- The fetus does contribute to its own development, and most movements present at birth will have already appeared (swallowing, breathing, limb movement, sleep-wake cycles). Movement is first felt by the mother around 16-20 weeks
- All five senses except visual are engaged within the fetus.
- Newborns prefer smells, tastes, and sounds that are familiar due to prenatal exposure, and are able to recognize rhymes and stories presented before birth, measured via increases in fetal heart rate. As such, culinary preferences can be transmitted via exposure in utero when the flavors are re-exposed during infancy and childhood
- However, education cannot, per se, be done in utero, as that would require higher order cognition in the form of understanding language

### Hazards to prenatal development
#### Miscarriage
- Defined as loss of a zygote/embryo/fetus before week 20. 
- 80% occur during the first trimester, and the rate in the second trimester is 1-5%. 
- The overall risk is 15% per pregnancy, with a 25% lifetime risk. It is modulated by parental and environmental factors.
##### Parental factors
- Age (older age of parents increases likelihood of genetic abnormalities as well as frequesncy of de novo mutations)
- Increased caloric burden on the mother (about 300 extra calories), with quality of food intake being an important factor (e.g. vitamin intake)
##### Teratogens
- Teratogenic influence is modulated by dose, duration, individual differences (genetic risk), and timing (sensitive periods). Examples include viral/bacterial infection and drugs such as alcohol. 
- The rate of fetal alcohol syndrome in the US is 1-5%, and heavy drinking greatly increases this risk.
- Some research suggests that light drinking does not lead to statistically significant decreases in infant cognition during pregnancy
- Teratogenic influences can be delayed, and may not be overt
- Teratogenic exposure is (of course) not always able to be controlled. Such exposure is mitigated by class and access to resources, among other factors.
