- Gray matter is composed of cell bodies and dendrites
- White matter include neuronal axons and their respective myelin sheaths (the sheath is produced by glial cells)
- Lobes of the brain, such as the occipital, temporal, parietal, and frontal lobes, roughly correspond to categories of behavior

### A more modern conceptualization of the brain
- The brain is a system of circuits that span the volume of the brain, and work in concert rather than forming discrete areas of functioning dedicated to specific behavioral/functional aspects
- The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex supports working memory (tied to executive function) and cognitive control. This was identified by researchers through fMRI scans of the brain while participants were performing a working memory task
- The dlPFC is part of the frontoparietal network along with the dorsolateral PFC and the posterior parietal cortex

### Brain development terms/processes
- Neurogenesis is largely complete by 18 weeks (?)
- Migration: The brain develops from inside out, and will have 6 layers by 7 months
- Synaptogenesis is mostly postnatal process
- Synaptic pruning does not necessarily involve apoptosis, but rather a weakening of unused synapses ("use it or lose it")
- Brain development occurs in the following order (roughly sequentially with overlaps): neurogenesis, migration, synaptogenesis, apoptosis, synaptic pruning, myelination, and circuit formation
- During migration, glial cells guide new neurons out of the inner brain to their intended locations, thus producing the inside-to-out process that is characteristic of fetal brain development
- The hippocampus continues neurogenesis past the fetal prenatal period
- Adolescence is a second developmental period in which overproduction and pruning is akin to that of the first years of life
- Gray matter increases throughout childhood, peaks in adolescence, and finally declines as some of the gray matter is replaced by white matter. Different regions peak at different times and change at different rates
- There is a steady increase in white matter from childhood to adulthood
- As a child grows to adulthood, greater separation develops between functional circuits, but correspondingly regions within the same functional circuit become more tightly linked. Essentially, the brain consolidates and tightens functional circuits to specialize

## Adverse experiences
- Research from Rakesh et al. (2021) shows that trauma delays the segregation of functional circuits in the brain, or in other words functional circuits remain linked contrary to the typical process which occurs in non-trauma exposed children
- Similarly, traumatic exposure induces greater connectivity within functional circuits, representing an adaptive response to trauma exposure

## Plasticity
- There are two types of neural plasticity

### Experience-expectant plasticity
- Plasticity that results from stimulation that virtually every human experiences. 
- Decreases the genetic demand for maturation and allows for greater adaptation given that nearly everyone receives those stimuli. 
- If exposure to the prevalent stimuli does *not* occur, maturation may be impacted or delayed, and there may be limits to how much one can "catch up" developmentally. 

### Experience-dependent plasticity
- Plasticitry from the varied experiences that humans have (different for each person)
- More complex environmental stimuli tend to correspond to more neural complexity and thus better performance on learning tasks

## Imaging
- EEG provides information on when things happen in the brain, whereas fMRI provides information on where it happens
- EEGs measure event-related potentials (ERPs) representing firing of large populations of neurons, which can be correlated to a stimulus
- Diffusion tensor imaging (DTI) creates maps of white matter in the brain. MRI similarly produces maps of the locations of gray and white matter in the brain
- Unfortuantely MRI is very sensitive to motion, making it less suitable for infants
- EEG is more motion friendly but primarily suitable for light colored hair
