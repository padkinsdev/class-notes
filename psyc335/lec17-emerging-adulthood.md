# Emerging Adulthood

## Introduction
- The developmental period between 18 and 25
- Coined as a term by Erikson, to describe a "prolonged adolescence"

### Normative challenges
- Many new experiences occur, such as employment, higher education, etc.
- The median number of job changes between ages 18 and 29 is 8
- The poverty rate remains high in emerging adulthood
- The goal of emerging adulthood is to complete identity formation and establish independence in multiple domains
- The exact ages and competencies of emerging adulthood vary across countries, cultures, and contexts
- In some cultures family interdependence is valued in place of or alongside independence
- Mental health challenges can arise as a result of challenges

### Features of emerging adulthood
- Ecological instability (economic, interpersonal)
- Identity exploration
- Self-focus
- Person-level instability
- Possibilities and optimism

## Psychopathology
- Schizophrenia and bipolar disorder tend to arise in emerging adulthood
- Depressive, anxiety, eating, and bipolar disorder emerge with notable frequency in this period
- Substance use increases in emerging adulthood, potentially with an increase in substance use disorder
- A minority of those with mental illnesses seek/obtain treatment, with cost playing a significant role in this

### Promotive factors
- Optimism remains high despite high rates of psychopathology
- Activism is a protective factor, with social analysis and sociopolitical action playing an important role in empowering emerging adults
- Sociopolitical action is especially important for marginalized communities
- Civic participation is associated with quicker recovery from mental health challenges, higher perceived qulity of life, greater overall health, and less substance use
- Only about 30% of emerging adults vote
