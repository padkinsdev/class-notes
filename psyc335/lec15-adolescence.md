# Adolescence

## Academic performance
- The riskiest times for dips in academic function occur at transitions (elementary/middle, middle/high school). 
- Protective factors include school engagement, academic self-rated beliefs and attitudes, and school resources
- Moderating factors include gender, race-ethnicity, and socioeconomic status

## Familial relationships
- Mother and father-child warmth tends to decrease during adolescence and then increase again at the transition to adulthood
- Frequency of parent-child conflict decreases, but affective intensity of said conflict increases. This is correlated with puberty and resulting hormonal surges

## Puberty
- Sex steroids organize neural development
- Pubertal development shapes external perceptions, expectations, and behavior
- Risk taking increases during puberty
    - Cognitive control system: several regions of the prefrontal cortex that undergird executivde functions and other higher=order self-regualtory processes necessary for top-down behavioral regulaqtion
    - Socioemotional system: neural regions underlying sensation seeking and emotion sensitivity
- The temporoparietal junction (TPJ), which corresponds with mentalizing and theory of mind, is more active during adolescence than adulthood
- Risk taking is now believed to consist of gradual development in the cognitive control system, socioemotional system, and social brain network
- Adolescence is effectively a second period of developmental sensitivity after infancy
