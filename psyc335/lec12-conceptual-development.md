# Conceptual Development

## Introduction
- Nativists argue that innate understanding of concepts plays a central role in development
- Empiricists argue that concepts arise from basic learning mechanisms
- Constructivism is a middle ground between nativism and empiricism
- Categorization enables generalizability even when object-specific knowledge is not present. Thus, children must develop the skill
- Basic/superordinate categories (e.g. "cat") are developed first, then subordinate categories (e.g. "tabby") are formed on top
- Children use scaffolding to build categories and differentiate between them. Thus a wealth of experience is beneficial for category development is necessary
- Around age 2 children increase categorization based on overall shapes, functions, and connections between actions and objects
- There is evidence to suggest that children (4-5 years old) build categories based on causal connections, i.e. an understanding of the function tied to a morphology

## People
- Naive psychology is a commonplace understanding of oneself or others
- Theory of mind refers to an organized understanding of how mental processes such as intentions influence behavior
- To demonstrate a full theory of mind, a child must show that they can predict a person's actions based on beliefs and desires
- Similarly, theory of mind involves the understanding that other people can have different beliefs and desires than oneself

### Theory of mind module
- Nativist
- Belief that theory of mind is innate, and the reason why children fail to display a theory of mind until 4-5 is because they need to develop other skills to sufficiently express it
- The existence of autism spectrum disorders is further used as evidence, with some autistic individuals displaying difficulty with joint attention and false belief understanding

### General cognitive abilities
- Empiricist
- Belief that the growth of domain-general information-processing skills are essential to theory of mind
- Executive function: children must develop the ability to suppress the belief that everyone has the same desires and beliefs as them
- It has been experimentally determined that executive function skills are not sufficient to produce a theory of mind

### Social interactions
- Empiricist
- Belief that children develop theory of mind by interacting and receiving feedback from others
- Evidenced by the escalating complexity of pretend play
- Children who have older siblings pass false belief tasks earlier than those who don't
