## Normal form games
- Game theory strives for an equilibrium, with the Nash equilibrium being the most important/prominent one
    - A Nash equilibrium is a position in a noncooperative game where no player can improve their expected outcome by changing their strategy (relative to the strategies of other players)
    - In other words, a Nash equilibrium is a point where each individual's strategy is optimal provided no one changes their strategy
    - An equilibrium takes all players into account
- Pure strategy: Choosing the same option 100% of the time
- Mixed strategy: Choosing different strategies with given probabilities (e.g. choosing option A 20% of the time and option B 80% of the time)
    - The payoff can be more complex to calculate in the case of a mixed strategy, but one approach is to weighted average the expected utilities of the involved strategies

## Probability
- Probability consists of an experiment, a sample space, an event (outcome), and the probability of that event (or a different event)
- Joint probability refers to the intersection of events, or in other words the probability that two events occur
- In game theory, the sample space is all of the action profiles of the players
- Two events are independent if $Pr(A \cap B) = Pr(A)Pr(B)$, or in other words if the outcome A doesn't affect the outcome B
- **Disjoint does not mean independent.** Two events are disjoint if they cannot both occur, but they are independent if their outcomes do not affect one another
    - Thus, two events cannot be both disjoint and independent
